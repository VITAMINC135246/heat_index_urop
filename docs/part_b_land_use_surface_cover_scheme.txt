Part B Land-Use and Surface-Cover Classification Scheme
=======================================================

Project: Heat Index UROP
Purpose: Reference for visible-image annotation, LUHK overlay, V-to-T mask
         transfer, and later temperature analysis
Created: 2026-06-16


1. Why two classification layers are required
---------------------------------------------

This project requires two related but independent classification layers.

Layer 1: Broad land use
Question: What is this area primarily used for in an urban-planning or
land-use context?

Examples include residential, commercial, industrial, and transport.
Broad land use describes the function of an area.

Layer 2: Surface cover
Question: What physical surface is actually visible at this image pixel?

Examples include building roof, asphalt, tree canopy, grass, and water.
Surface cover describes the visible physical material or feature.

Broad land use and surface cover are related, but they are not the same
concept. One broad land-use area can contain several surface-cover classes.

Example:

Broad land use: Residential

Possible surface covers:
- Building roof
- Asphalt
- Concrete pavement
- Tree canopy
- Low vegetation


2. LUHK limitation and official-use rationale
---------------------------------------------

Official LUHK documentation describes LUHK as a broad-brush representation of land use. Its raster resolution is 10 metres, and the dataset is not designed for detailed analysis or precise calculation.

A 10 m × 10 m LUHK raster cell represents approximately 100 m². One cell may
contain buildings, vegetation, roads, paved ground, and other physical
surfaces at the same time. Therefore, one LUHK class must not be treated as
the pixel-level surface-cover label for a drone image.

LUHK should be used as broad land-use context. Surface cover must be annotated
independently from the high-resolution visible drone imagery.


3. Layer 1: LUHK broad land-use categories
------------------------------------------

The ten current LUHK source categories must be retained without early merging:

1. Residential
2. Commercial
3. Industrial
4. GIC / open space
5. Transport
6. Other urban / built-up land
7. Agriculture
8. Woodland / shrubland / grassland / wetland
9. Barren land
10. Water bodies

These categories provide broad land-use context. Grouped categories may be
created later for statistical analysis, but they must not overwrite or delete
the original LUHK categories.

Recommended fields:

luhk_class_raw
    The original LUHK category.

luhk_class_grouped
    An optional later grouping used for a specific analysis.


4. Layer 2: Drone surface-cover categories
------------------------------------------

The pilot-stage surface-cover classes are:

0. Unknown / excluded
   Areas that cannot be classified reliably; severely blurred or occluded
   areas; image edges or invalid regions after registration; and vehicles,
   pedestrians, temporary objects, or other objects not currently included in
   the stable surface classification.

1. Building roof
   Building roofs visible from the overhead drone view. Roofs are not further
   divided by material during the pilot stage.

2. Asphalt
   Asphalt roads, asphalt parking areas, and similar dark paved surfaces.

3. Concrete / paved surface
   Concrete, brick, plazas, footpaths, and other hard paved surfaces that are
   not asphalt.

4. Tree canopy
   Tree crowns or clearly elevated woody vegetation actually visible in the
   drone image.

5. Low vegetation
   Grass, low shrubs, and other low-growing vegetation.

6. Bare soil / rock
   Exposed soil, exposed rock, unpaved ground, and natural surfaces without
   clear vegetation cover.

7. Water
   Sea, pools, rivers, and other visible water surfaces.

The pilot stage deliberately uses a limited number of classes to improve
annotation consistency, reduce subjective differences between annotators, and
avoid splitting limited samples into many small categories. Classes may be
refined later when there is sufficient evidence. A coarse scheme is easier to
reproduce and can be consistently regrouped.


5. Shadow is a separate layer
-----------------------------

Shadow is not a surface-cover class.

Shadow describes illumination condition, not physical surface material.

For example:

surface_cover = Asphalt
shadow = 1

or:

surface_cover = Asphalt
shadow = 0

A separate binary shadow mask is recommended:

0 = not shadowed
1 = shadowed

Shadow must not be mixed as a mutually exclusive class with building roof,
asphalt, tree canopy, or the other surface-cover classes.


6. Handling mixed LUHK cells
----------------------------

The relationship between LUHK raster cells and drone pixels is many-to-many.
For example, one LUHK cell may have:

luhk_class_raw = Residential

while its surface composition is:

Building roof: 45%
Tree canopy: 25%
Asphalt: 20%
Concrete / paved surface: 10%

Analysis must not force one LUHK cell to correspond to only one surface-cover
class. For each LUHK cell or analysis window, later processing should consider
calculating:

roof_fraction
asphalt_fraction
concrete_fraction
tree_fraction
low_vegetation_fraction
bare_ground_fraction
water_fraction
shadow_fraction

dominant_surface_cover may also be saved for display or simplified analysis,
but the dominant class must not replace the complete class fractions.


7. Handling V-to-T resolution conversion
-----------------------------------------

The surface-cover mask is first annotated on the high-resolution visible
image. It is then mapped by geometric registration to the thermal image's
640 × 512 grid.

One thermal pixel may cover several visible-image pixels, so the converted
thermal representation may contain mixed surface cover. The pilot stage should
retain at least:

1. A majority-class thermal mask.
2. Surface-cover fraction information where practical.

For example, if one thermal pixel corresponds to:

70% Tree canopy
30% Asphalt

the majority mask may classify it as Tree canopy. Where technically practical,
the class proportions should also be preserved to avoid losing the mixed
surface information.


8. Annotation principles
------------------------

1. Annotate the surface actually visible to the drone; do not infer the ground
   beneath an occluding object.
2. Where tree canopy blocks a road, label the visible area as Tree canopy, not
   as road.
3. Use Unknown / excluded when a reliable decision cannot be made.
4. Do not guess simply to fill every part of the mask.
5. Never modify or overwrite original imagery.
6. Save annotations in a project-generated data directory, never in data/raw/.
7. Use the same category IDs consistently across all pilot images.
8. When the classification scheme changes, record its version and the reason
   for the change.


9. Intended final analytical structure
--------------------------------------

Each thermal pixel or analysis region may eventually have:

temperature
delta_temperature
surface_cover
shadow_status
broad_land_use

Example:

Temperature: 42.1 °C
Surface cover: Asphalt
Shadow: No
Broad land use: Transport

This structure supports analysis of:

- Temperature differences among surface-cover classes.
- Differences for the same surface cover across broad land-use contexts.
- The effect of shadow on the same surface-cover class.
- Relationships between within-cell surface composition and average
  temperature or ΔT.


10. Classification version
--------------------------

Classification scheme version: 1.0
Status: Pilot-stage definition

This scheme may be revised after inspecting the first pilot image and
evaluating annotation ambiguity. Any revision should preserve the previous
version or record a clear change log.
